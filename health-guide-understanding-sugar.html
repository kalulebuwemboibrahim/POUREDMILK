Understanding Sugar.
Sugar does more than make cake sweet.

THE IDEA

When comparing recipes, look at the whole ingredient list and serving size. A cake can still be a dessert even when it uses an alternative sweetener, and “sugar-free” does not automatically mean healthier.

IN DETAIL

A calmer, more useful way to think about cake.

When comparing recipes, look at the whole ingredient list and serving size. A cake can still be a dessert even when it uses an alternative sweetener, and “sugar-free” does not automatically mean healthier.

Sugar affects sweetness, browning, tenderness and moisture. Reducing it changes the structure and sensory experience of a cake, so a recipe cannot always be “fixed” by simply removing half the sugar. Start with your ordinary routine rather than trying to create a special set of rules just for dessert.

Practical approach

  • Look at the wider pattern of your meals, activity, sleep and hydration.
  • Choose a portion intentionally and enjoy it without rushing.
  • Keep your normal routine before and after the treat.
  • Avoid punishment, guilt or extreme compensation.
  • Adapt advice to your own needs rather than treating general guidance as a prescription.

What this guide is not

It is not a weight-loss prescription, a medical treatment or a promise that a particular ingredient makes cake “healthy”. It is a framework for thinking about enjoyment and balance.
