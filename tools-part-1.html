Scaling recipes

Learn the mathematics behind changing a batch size. Understand the basic multiplier, pan geometry, practical limits and when a simple scale-up stops being reliable.

01 / DEEP DIVE

Scale with a multiplier

The basic scaling factor is target yield divided by original yield. Multiply each ingredient by that factor, then check whether the pan shape and depth still make sense.

Very large changes can alter mixing, heat penetration and baking time. Scaling a recipe is arithmetic first, but baking physics still gets the final word.

POUREDMILK principle

Don't only memorise the instruction. Understand the reason behind it, then use that understanding when the situation changes.

What to look for

  • Identify the purpose of each major step.
  • Separate reliable principles from personal preference.
  • Observe the batter, crumb, heat and timing instead of relying on one number.
  • Record useful changes so your next bake becomes easier to predict.
