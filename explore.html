EXPLORE / THE CAKE ATLAS

The cake map
starts here.

Cake is not one thing. It is a family of formulas, textures, ingredients and traditions. Start broad, then follow whatever makes you curious.

01 / CAKE TYPES

Different formulas.
Different personalities.

BUTTER CAKES

Rich and structured.

Often built around butter or another fat, sugar, eggs and flour. They reward careful mixing and are excellent teachers.

FOAM CAKES

Air is the architecture.

Whipped eggs provide much of the lift. Genoise, angel food and roulade-style sponges show different versions of the idea.

CHIFFON

Light, but not fragile.

Oil keeps the crumb tender while whipped whites contribute lift.

POUND CAKES

Dense by design.

A high proportion of butter, sugar, eggs and flour creates a rich, tight crumb that keeps well.

CHEESECAKE

Cake as baked custard.

Cream cheese, eggs, sugar and heat create a smooth, dense structure rather than a flour-led crumb.

YEASTED CAKES

Where bread meets celebration.

Enriched doughs with sugar, eggs, butter or dried fruit form a cake-like family of their own.

02 / AROUND THE WORLD

Cake changes
with place.

BRITAIN

Tea-time traditions

Fruit cakes, Victoria-style sponge and loaf cakes show how cake became tied to afternoon tea, home baking and social ritual.

ITALY

Regional sweetness

Panettone, torta di mele and other cakes let fruit, nuts, citrus and dairy shape the personality of the bake.

JAPAN

Lightness and precision

Castella, airy cheesecakes and cream cakes show a strong preference for softness, balance and restrained sweetness.

WEST AFRICA

Spice and adaptation

Celebration cakes often combine familiar formulas with local preferences for spice, cocoa, coconut and richer textures.

LATIN AMERICA

Milk, fruit and celebration

Tres leches, corn-based cakes and fruit-forward desserts show how dairy, maize and tropical produce reshape cake.

SOUTH ASIA

Spice and hospitality

Cardamom, saffron, semolina, nuts and dairy create cakes and cake-like sweets connected to tea and celebration.

03 / INGREDIENTS

Every ingredient
has a job.

FLOUR

Structure.

Flour supplies starch and proteins. Hydrated proteins can form gluten, which helps hold the cake together.

EGGS

Structure + richness.

Eggs bring water, protein, fat and emulsifiers. They can trap air, set the crumb and add colour.

SUGAR

More than sweetness.

Sugar attracts water, tenderizes, supports browning and helps aerate fat during creaming.

FAT

Tenderness.

Butter and oils limit gluten development and create a softer mouthfeel while carrying flavour.

LIQUID

Activation.

Liquid hydrates flour, dissolves sugar and gives the batter its workable consistency.

LEAVENERS

Gas and lift.

Baking soda and powder release gases that expand in the oven and contribute to volume.
